Lucky numbers for business

How shops, restaurants, agents and brands use 8s, and avoid 4s, to win Chinese-speaking customers.

Updated September 2026 · 7 min read

Quick answer: end prices in 8 (or 88 / 168), keep 4 out of prices, phone numbers, unit and floor numbers, and open on a 成 / 开 / 满 / 定 day that doesn't clash with the owner's sign.

1. Pricing

In Chinese markets 8 is the charm digit, doing the job that 9 does in the West. Banquet menus are tiered at 1,688 / 2,888 / 3,888. Online sales cluster at 66.66 and 88.88. Anything with a 4 (499, 1,444) quietly underperforms.

2. Phone numbers

A business line ending in 8, 88, 168 or 6688 is marketing in its own right: customers remember it and read it as a good sign. Carriers in Greater China and Singapore sell these “pretty numbers” at premiums, and resale markets price them by pattern.

3. Addresses & floors

Many buildings skip 4, 14 and 24 as floor labels. Units ending in 8 command premiums, while 4-heavy addresses can take longer to lease. If you can choose, pick 8, 18, 28, 68 or 88.

4. Launch dates

Openings are traditionally set by the almanac. Look for 成 (Success), 开 (Open) or 满 (Full) days, make sure the day doesn't clash with the owner's sign, and prefer dates with 8 in them (8 August is the classic). Find opening days →

5. Names, codes and SKUs

Promo codes like LUCKY88 or 168OFF land better than SAVE40. Check product codes, table numbers and ticket numbers too, because customers notice.

Checklist

  • ☐ No 4 in prices, phone, unit, promo codes
  • ☐ Hero price ends in 8
  • ☐ Opening on a 成/开/满/定 day, no owner clash
  • ☐ Red and gold used for the launch, white avoided

FAQ

Does lucky pricing work outside Asia?

Yes, with Chinese-speaking diaspora customers in Vancouver, Toronto, Sydney, London, New York and elsewhere. For mixed audiences an 8 ending is harmless and still reads as a charm price.

What is the luckiest business phone ending?

8888 is the gold standard. 168, 518, 6688 and 888 are strong and more affordable.
